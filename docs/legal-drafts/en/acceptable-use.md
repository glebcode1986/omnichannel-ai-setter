## 1. What this Policy is

1.1. This Acceptable Use Policy governs what {{PRODUCT_NAME}} (the "**Service**") may be used for. It forms
part of the Terms of Service.

1.2. It exists because of what the Service does: it sends messages **to other people** — your customers —
over accounts you authorize, under platform rules we cannot override. A breach costs you your channel
before it costs us an argument.

## 2. The platforms' rules come with your authorization

2.1. When you connect an Instagram or WhatsApp Business account, that platform's policies apply to how it
may be used, and they apply to you.

2.2. **We cannot override them and we will not try.** Where a platform's rules forbid a message, the
Service declines to send it. That is not a fault.

2.3. A platform may change its rules, restrict your account or withdraw access for reasons of its own. Keep
your own channel in good standing; we cannot restore it for you.

## 3. The Service answers people who wrote to you first

3.1. **Do not use the Service to initiate contact with anybody who has not messaged you.**

3.2. The Service provides no bulk sending, no contact import for outreach, and no way to start a
conversation with somebody who has not started one. This is how the product is built, not only a rule
here. If you find yourself trying to work around it, you are using the wrong tool.

3.3. Do not use the Service for unsolicited commercial messaging, for messaging lists you bought or
scraped, or in any way that applicable marketing or consumer-protection law forbids.

## 4. What must not be sent

4.1. Do not use the Service to send:

   (a) anything unlawful, or anything promoting unlawful activity;
   (b) harassment, threats, abuse, or content inciting violence or hatred;
   (c) deceptive claims, false urgency, invented discounts, fabricated testimonials, or statements about
       price, availability or outcomes that you know to be untrue;
   (d) content impersonating another business or person, or suggesting an endorsement you do not have;
   (e) requests for passwords, card numbers, one-time codes or other credentials;
   (f) malware, or links intended to deceive;
   (g) sexual content involving minors, or any content exploiting a child — in any circumstances;
   (h) content infringing somebody's intellectual property or other rights.

4.2. The assistant is built not to invent prices, policies, availability, discounts, outcomes or
commitments. **Do not configure it to do so.** Knowledge and writing-style material you supply is treated
as trusted, which means the responsibility for what it causes the assistant to say is yours.

## 5. Consent, objections and opt-outs

5.1. **You are responsible for having a lawful basis for communicating with each person you message, and
for any consent that basis requires.** We act on your instructions; we cannot know how a contact reached
you.

5.2. **Honour requests to stop.** Where you record an objection in the Service, the Service keeps that
objection even if you later delete everything else about the person, because forgetting somebody and
remembering never to message them are not compatible and the objection is the part that protects them.

5.3. Tell the people who write to you what your own privacy obligations require, including, where
applicable, that their messages are handled with automated assistance. The AI Disclosure is written so you
may refer to it or reuse it.

5.4. Do not use the Service to process special categories of personal data as a matter of design. A person
may disclose something sensitive in a message, because people do; building a process that collects such
data through the Service is a different thing, and needs its own basis.

## 6. Your knowledge and style material

6.1. Supply only material you are entitled to use.

6.2. Do not use another business's copy, brand assets or customer data as your own knowledge or style
material.

6.3. Knowledge and writing-style material created at Workspace level is read by **every** Business Profile
in the Workspace. Creating one from a single profile's screen is a cross-Workspace act, which is why it is
reserved to a Workspace's owner or administrators. Treat it accordingly.

## 7. Interference with the assistant

7.1. Do not attempt to make the Service disregard its own safety rules, reveal credentials or internal
configuration, or send through a transport a platform does not permit.

7.2. The Service treats messages from your customers, retrieved knowledge and external content as material
to read rather than as instructions to follow. **Do not attempt to defeat that boundary**, whether through
your own configuration or by any other means.

7.3. Do not probe, scan, overload or reverse-engineer the Service, and do not try to reach another
Workspace's data.

## 8. Account integrity

8.1. One Workspace per business arrangement. Do not create multiple Workspaces to obtain more trials, more
capacity or more included allowance than your Plan provides.

8.2. Do not share a single member seat between people. Seats are counted because they are sold.

8.3. Keep sign-in credentials secure. A sign-in link is a credential: anyone holding it can sign in.

8.4. Do not resell or provide the Service to a third party as your own, unless we have agreed it in
writing.

## 9. Autopilot is your decision, and your responsibility

9.1. Autopilot sends messages to your customers **without a person reading them first**. You chose it; the
messages are yours.

9.2. Use Draft mode until you are satisfied with the assistant's behaviour on your own material. Review
what the Review Queue holds rather than letting it accumulate: it exists to put a person in front of the
cases that need one.

9.3. Do not use Autopilot for conversations you know fall into a high-risk category — payment disputes,
legal questions, medical or other high-risk advice, security matters. The Service holds those for a person
by design, and that design is not a substitute for your judgement about where to switch automation on.

10.2. If you believe content infringes your rights, write to {{LEGAL_EMAIL}} with enough detail to

## 11. What we do about a breach

11.1. Depending on what the circumstances require, we may: ask you to stop; restrict a feature; suspend
automated sending; suspend a Business Profile or the Workspace; or terminate the agreement.

11.2. **We will restrict no more than the circumstances require, and we will tell you what we have done.**
Receiving and storing messages your customers send continues even where automated activity is suspended —
a dispute about your use of the Service must not lose somebody's message.

11.3. Where the law requires a report, or a platform requires action, we will comply.

11.4. Suspension for a breach of this Policy does not ordinarily entitle you to a refund — see the Refund
and Cancellation Policy, clause 8.1(f).
