## 1. Overview, and who this is about

1.1. This Policy explains how {{SELLER_LEGAL_NAME}} ("**we**") handles personal data in connection with
{{PRODUCT_NAME}} (the "**Service**"), a platform that helps a business answer the people who message it on
Instagram and WhatsApp.

1.2. **Two different groups of people appear in this Policy, and we stand in a different relationship to
each.**

   (a) **Our customers and their members** — the businesses that subscribe, and the people who sign in and
       use the Service. For them we decide what is processed and why. We are the **controller**.
   (b) **The people who message our customers** — their customers, who wrote to a business on Instagram or
       WhatsApp and have never visited us. For them our customer decides what happens, and we act on that
       customer's instructions. We are a **processor**, and our customer is the controller.

1.3. If you messaged a business and want to know what it holds about you, or want it erased, **the business
is who to ask.** We cannot make that decision for them, and we will not act on it without their
instruction. Clause 14.4 says what we will do.

## 3. What we process

3.1. **Account and identity data.** Name and e-mail address; sign-in method; the identifier a sign-in
provider gives us where you sign in with one; session records; interface language.

3.2. **Workspace data.** Workspace and Business Profile records, members and their roles, settings, and the
knowledge and writing-style material a customer configures.

3.3. **Channel authorizations.** The authorization a customer grants for an Instagram or WhatsApp account,
its scope and expiry, and the account's own identifiers on the platform. The credential itself is a secret
and is handled under clause 15.

3.4. **Conversation content.** What a person wrote; media and voice notes they sent; what the Service
prepared and what was sent; who decided to send it; delivery and read state; and timestamps.

3.5. **Transcripts.** Where transcription is enabled, the words of a voice note as a transcription provider
reads them. A transcript is a reading of a recording and can be wrong. Where a person typed a caption on
the same message, their own words take precedence over the machine's reading of their voice.

3.6. **Derived data.** Conversation summaries, facts and preferences the Service records about a person,
writing-style examples, lead stage, and the numerical search representations of any of it. **Derived data
is personal data about the same person.** A product that deleted the correspondence and kept the summary
has not forgotten anybody, and this Policy does not pretend otherwise.

3.7. **Billing data.** Plan, interval, currency, amounts, subscription state, payment and refund state, and
the identifiers a payment provider gives us. **We never receive or store full card numbers.**

3.8. **Operational data.** Queue jobs, outbound delivery records, duplicate-suppression ledgers and audit
events. These carry identifiers, outcomes and counts — **never message bodies**.

3.9. **Technical data.** IP address, request metadata and error diagnostics, for security and for finding
faults.

3.10. We do not ask for special categories of personal data. A person writing to a business may
nonetheless disclose something sensitive in a message, because people do. Our customer decides what to do
about that; we process what their conversations contain.

## 5. Why we process it

5.1. To provide the Service: receiving messages, assembling the context for a reply, preparing a reply,
applying safety rules, queuing and delivering what is sent, and keeping the record.

5.2. To transcribe voice notes, where that is enabled and an allowance remains.

5.3. To retrieve relevant knowledge and style material, which requires computing and comparing search
representations of text.

5.4. To operate accounts, subscriptions, capacity, allowances, invoicing, refunds and disputes.

5.5. To keep a record of who decided what: which person approved or edited a reply, what was sent, and on

5.6. To keep the Service secure and to prevent abuse, including duplicate-suppression, rate limiting and
investigating faults.

5.7. To communicate with you about your account — most importantly to send a sign-in link when you ask for
one. **There is no marketing mail in the Service.**

5.8. To meet legal obligations, including accounting and tax record-keeping, and to establish or defend

## 7. Automated assistance and AI providers

7.1. The Service prepares replies using third-party models. To do so it sends the model what it needs for
that one reply: a bounded part of the conversation, the knowledge and style material retrieved for it, and
a transcript where one exists.

7.2. **What is sent is bounded and deliberate.** The model is given a structured set of labelled pieces
rather than one assembled block of text. Content that did not come from the customer's own configuration
is treated as untrusted: it is cleaned, limited in length, and marked so the model treats it as material to
read rather than as instructions to follow. The history window is bounded and retrieval results are
capped. **Nothing reaches a model that was not read for that reply, and a credential never reaches a model
at all.**

7.3. The categories of provider involved are: a provider that prepares text; a provider that transcribes
audio, where transcription is enabled; and a provider that computes search representations, where that is
enabled. The Service is built so that a provider can be replaced, and the current list is maintained with
the subprocessor arrangements rather than written into this Policy, so that a change is a notified change
rather than a silent edit to a web page.

7.4. **A note on model training.** This Policy does not state that content sent to a provider is excluded
from training that provider's models, because that depends on each provider's terms and on how the account
is configured, and we will not claim it until it is verified for each provider. See
[`../OWNER_DECISIONS.md`](../OWNER_DECISIONS.md), D-14.

7.5. **Automated decisions.** The Service does not use personal data to make automated decisions with
legal or similarly significant effects. It drafts and sends messages. Where a case falls into a category
the Service treats as high-risk — payment disputes, legal questions, high-risk advice, security matters,
serious complaints, unusual commitments, or a missing material fact — it holds the reply for a person
instead of sending it.

## 8. Human review and takeover

8.1. A person can take over any conversation at any time, and when they do, automated sending for that
conversation stops until it is resumed.

8.2. When a person edits a prepared reply before approving it, the Service may keep their final wording as
an example of the customer's writing style. It is kept as a style example and **not** as a business fact.

8.3. Each decision a person makes — approve, edit and send, dismiss, claim — is recorded with the
decision, the person, the wording and the authority they granted, as one record. This is why a disputed
message can be traced to a decision rather than to a system.

## 12. Deletion

12.1. Deletion is requested in one of four scopes, chosen when the request is made and never widened
afterwards:

| Scope | What it removes | What it keeps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One person | The person, their conversations, and everything derived from them | A recorded objection to being contacted, as described in 12.4 |
| One conversation | That conversation, and what was derived from it | The person, and what was learned in their other conversations |
| One Business Profile | Every person in it | The profile itself |
| A Workspace | Every person in every profile | Records the law, a regulator or a payment provider requires |

12.2. A deletion runs as a durable process and finishes in one of two states: completed, or **partially
failed**. A partial failure is final rather than retried on a schedule, because a deletion that removed
some things and not others must not be reported as done.

12.3. **The platform's copy goes first.** A media identifier is the only handle we have on a recording held
by the messaging platform, so the request to delete it is made before the record naming it is removed. If
that request cannot be made, the deletion is recorded as a partial failure — a deletion reported as
completed while a voice note remained on somebody else's servers would be untrue.

12.4. **An objection to contact is kept, and it is not a deletion.** You cannot both erase somebody and
remember never to message them. Where a person has asked not to be contacted, the record of that
objection and the platform's own identifier for them are kept, while their names, contact details,
correspondence and derived data are removed. That is the least that honours the request they actually
made.

12.5. **What a deletion cannot remove.** Records of consent to our legal documents, and the billing and
accounting records the law requires us to keep, are not removed by a deletion request. Where a right to
erasure applies, it is limited by the legal obligation that requires the record — and that limit is stated
here rather than discovered later.

## 15. Security

15.1. Channel credentials are encrypted at rest with the Workspace, the credential's identity and its
purpose bound into the encryption, so that a record cannot be decrypted in the wrong context. The
plaintext exists only in memory, between being read and being used.

15.2. **Nothing logs a secret.** Error reporting passes through an allow-list of fields and deliberately
avoids the place where HTTP clients expose request configuration, which is where a token would otherwise
appear. A sign-in link is treated the same way: it is a credential for one click, and it is never written
to a log.

15.3. Authorization is checked on every request against the member's current role and the Business Profiles
they may see, and is not cached in a session — so a revoked role takes effect at once. Scope alone is not
permission: reading a conversation and taking the whole of somebody's history out of the Service are
different capabilities.

15.4. Taking the whole of a person's history out, erasing it, or erasing what the Service has learned about
them are reserved to a Workspace's owner or administrators.
